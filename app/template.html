How to make it stick

The research on durable learning keeps landing on two techniques: retrieval practice (pulling information out of memory) and spaced practice (coming back to it over increasing gaps). Re-reading and highlighting feel productive but rank low for long-term retention. Semiologie is mostly lists of signs, their causes, and pairs you have to tell apart, which suits these methods well.

The session loop (use it for every new subchapter)

  1. 1
    Pre-test2 min
    Before reading, look at the subchapter heading and write what you think is in it. Guessing wrong first still improves what you remember afterwards.
  2. 2
    Read one chunk with a question5–8 pages
    Read actively. For every sign, ask "what would make me suspect this, and what else looks like it?" Don't highlight.
  3. 3
    Close the book and blurt5–10 min
    On a blank page, write or say aloud everything you remember from the chunk. Then open the book and mark what you missed in red. The red items are what you study next. Use the Recall tab for this.
  4. 4
    Turn gaps into cards5 min
    One fact per card. Keep each card asking for one thing ("Weber in transmission deafness → ?") rather than a whole list. Long lists become several cards or a cloze.
  5. 5
    Explain the mechanismwhile you write cards
    Tie each sign to its cause: why does central cyanoza from lung failure correct with O₂ but cyanogenic heart disease doesn't? A fact attached to a reason is much harder to lose.
  6. 6
    Review on schedule20–30 min/day
    The deck schedules each card at roughly 1 → 3 → 7 → 16 → 35+ days, stretching the gap each time you get it right. Do the reviews before you read anything new.
  7. 7
    Mix, don't block2–3×/week
    Use mixed clinical cases (Cases tab) that jump between fever, facies, eyes and consciousness. Mixing forces you to choose which concept applies, and that choice is what the exam and the ward test.
  8. 8
    Draw it, do it, teach itweekly
    Sketch the 10 fever curves and the 3 coma postures from memory. Do Rinne/Weber, the visual field exam and the thyroid palpation on a colleague. Once a week, explain one subchapter out loud as if teaching a year-I student, without notes.

For Chapter I (p. 7–76)

  • Daily: introduce 25–30 new cards a day. The deck has all cards, so all of them are in rotation in about 10 days. If you have an exam soon, raise the limit to 40–50.
  • After each section's cards: read its page in the Explanations tab. Understanding the mechanism makes the next review easier.
  • Day 1: do the blurt sheets for Anamneza and Febra. Compare them with the checklists and add any missed items to your own notes.
  • Day 3: do the Cases tab. Re-read only the pages behind the questions you missed.
  • Day 7: fill in the contrast tables from memory on paper, then check them against the Recall tab.

For the rest of the book (p. 77 → 263, starting with the respiratory system)

  • Daily: due reviews first (they'll settle around 30–50 cards), then one new chunk with the loop above. At about 10 pages a day, the remaining ~185 pages take 3 weeks.
  • Weekly: one mixed-case session, one teach-back, and one hand-drawn diagram per system (posture, curve, maneuver).
  • Before the exam: do not switch to re-reading. Keep doing reviews and cases, and blurt every chapter heading one more time. Sleep the night before. Memory consolidates during sleep, so an all-nighter costs you retention.
  • If an exam date is fixed, place your last new chunk at least 10 days before it, so every card gets 2–3 more reviews.

Using the exam questions

  • After a section's cards, practise that chapter in the Exam questions tab with feedback after each question. Read every note: the notes mark the places where the old banks disagree with your course.
  • Once a week, run a 30–50 question exam simulation across all chapters you've covered. Then repeat "only my mistakes" until that list is empty.
  • The Chișinău bank and the Grile 3-x sets come from other courses. The Chișinău bank uses some different terms and keys, so where it conflicts with the 2022 course, learn the course version.

Card-writing rules

  • One question, one answer. "Cauzele febrei continue?" is a good card. "Toate tipurile de febră?" is not.
  • Build cards around discriminators: ptoză + mioză vs ptoză + midriază. The exam tests the difference between similar signs.
  • Number thresholds get their own card (37,2 / 37,7; 3–5 mm; 80/94 cm).
  • Use mnemonics only for arbitrary lists, e.g. ABCDE for melanom. Use the mechanism for everything else.
  • If a card fails 4+ times, rewrite it smaller or add the "why". Don't keep grinding the same wording.
